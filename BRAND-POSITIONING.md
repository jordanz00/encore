# Brand Positioning — Artist-First, Open Source, in One Sentence

> Every public surface of Encore — landing page, press kit, App Store listing eventually, founder bio — uses the messaging in this document. Consistency is the brand.

## The one-sentence pitch

> **Encore is the artist-owned music platform: sell your music directly, stream it everywhere, federate it to the fediverse, and keep every penny you earn — built open source, by artists, for artists.**

## The 30-second pitch

> Streaming is dead money. Spotify pays $0.003 a stream. Bandcamp just laid off half its staff. Audius lost $1.1M to a hack. None of them are run by artists, for artists.
>
> Encore is the artist-owned alternative — open source under AGPL-3.0, owned by a non-profit foundation, federated via ActivityPub so artists on Encore can be followed and tipped from Mastodon. We charge zero platform fee on direct sales. We use a user-centric subscription pool so your fans' money flows only to you, not a global pot that subsidizes megastars. We pay artists everywhere — Nigeria, Pakistan, Argentina — through Wise, M-Pesa, UPI, and Pix, not just to PayPal-supported countries.
>
> If you have written a song, recorded a podcast, mixed a DJ set, or want to support the people who do — this is where it lives.

## The two-minute pitch

A longer version for press, founder interviews, and the launch blog post.

### Section 1 — The problem (40s)

> The music industry generates more revenue than ever and pays artists worse than ever. Spotify pays $0.003 per stream, and as of April 2024 your track has to clear 1,000 streams in a rolling 12-month window or it earns *nothing.* 87% of all tracks on Spotify earn zero. The platform reclassified Premium as a "bundle" with audiobooks in March 2024 — that single accounting move cost songwriters $150 million in mechanical royalties in 12 months. Discovery Mode is under a payola class action lawsuit. Investigative reporting in Harper's exposed "Perfect Fit Content" — Spotify commissions music from ~20 songwriters and distributes it across 500+ ghost artist profiles on the biggest mood playlists to dilute payouts.
>
> Spotify's CEO recently invested a billion dollars in a military AI drone company. Musicians are walking off the platform — Deerhoof, King Gizzard, Massive Attack, Godspeed You! Black Emperor — and they need somewhere to go.

### Section 2 — Why every alternative has fallen short (40s)

> Bandcamp was the artist-friendly alternative. Epic Games bought it, then sold it to Songtradr in October 2023, and Songtradr laid off about half the staff including the entire union committee. Audius tried tokens and decentralization — speculator capture, $1.1M governance hack, the token is down 70%. Tidal tried user-centric payouts and the major labels blocked it. Sound.xyz and Catalog tried music NFTs and both shut down in early 2026. Funkwhale, the open-source federated peer, survives on NLnet grants but has no clear path from listener to artist revenue, so artists have no reason to upload.
>
> The pattern: every prior attempt died for *non-technical* reasons — no funding plan, no legal entity, speculator capture, label veto, acquisition-and-gut. We designed Encore to be immune to each of those failure modes.

### Section 3 — What Encore is (40s)

> Encore is open-source under AGPL-3.0 — so no one can fork it into a closed SaaS. It will be owned by a non-profit foundation, not a venture-backed startup — so no investor exit risk. It is federated via ActivityPub — so artists on Encore can be followed from Mastodon and Funkwhale, and so single instances can serve specific regions, scenes, and communities. It charges zero platform fee on direct sales — better than Bandcamp's 10–15%. It pays subscription royalties user-centric — your $9.99 flows only to artists *you* played, not to a global pool that pays Drake your subsidy. It pays artists everywhere — Nigeria, Pakistan, Argentina, Brazil — through Wise, M-Pesa, MTN MoMo, UPI, Pix, and Lightning, because Western platforms refuse to. It has a real DDEX delivery pipeline so distributors like Amuse and RouteNote can deliver to it the same way they deliver to Spotify.
>
> And it ships as one tool that consolidates Bandcamp + Patreon + SoundCloud + DistroKid + a fan email list, instead of asking working artists to juggle five vendors.

## The four pillars (visual landing page sections)

The marketing site at `encore.audio` is built around these four pillars. Each pillar has a 3–4 word headline, a 1-line subhead, and a 2–3 sentence body.

### Pillar 1 — Artist-First

> **Built around what artists actually earn.**
>
> 0% platform fee on direct sales, tipping, and merch. User-centric subscription pool — your fans' money flows to you. No 1,000-stream cliff. Songwriters get a per-track mechanical royalty dashboard. Right-to-export your catalog, fan list, and sales receipts at any time, forever.

> **AGPL-3.0. Built in the open. Un-acquirable.**
>
> Every line of code is open source. The roadmap is public. Governance progresses to a non-profit foundation — no investor can buy us, gut the staff, and call it "synergies." If we ever break our promises to artists, the AGPL fork is the real Encore.

>
> ActivityPub-native. Artists on Encore can be followed from any Mastodon or Funkwhale instance. Communities, labels, festivals, and scenes can self-host their own instance — same software, full federation, no permission required. Subsonic-API compatible, so it works with the 50+ existing self-hosted-music mobile apps day 1.

### Pillar 4 — Global

> **For artists everywhere, not just California.**
>
> Pays artists in Nigeria, Kenya, India, Brazil, Indonesia, Argentina — through Wise, M-Pesa, MTN MoMo, UPI, Pix, MercadoPago, Lightning. ## Voice and tone

Encore communicates:

- **Direct, not promotional.** No "Welcome to the future of music" energy. Every sentence is testable.
- **Cited.** Every claim about competitors links to a primary source. We can defend every number.
- **Plain English, 8th–10th grade reading level** for marketing copy. Technical depth lives in the strategy docs.
- **Respectful to artists, factual about platforms.** We criticize Spotify's business model — not Spotify employees. We criticize Songtradr's layoffs — not the engineers who got laid off.
- **Anti-hype on technology.** No "AI-powered," no "blockchain," no "Web3." If a feature does not exist yet, we say "v0.x roadmap." If a claim is uncertain, we say "target."
- **Optimistic about artists.** The hard truth about platforms must be matched with the believable claim that something better can exist.

## Words and phrases banned

| Banned phrase | Why |
|---|---|
| "Spotify killer" | Wrong frame — we're not in the Spotify-replacement business; we're the artist-business-hub |
| "Web3 music" | The category is in collapse and the word is contaminated |
| "AI-powered" | Indie artists are explicitly leaving over AI-flooded platforms |
| "Monetize your art" | Reductive; we say "earn from your work" |
| "Fan engagement" | Marketing speak; we say "what fans pay for and listen to" |
| "Disrupting the music industry" | Cliché; we say specifically what we change |
| "100% royalty retention" | Confusing — every platform has processor fees. We say "0% platform fee, processor fee only" |
| "Decentralized" | We say "federated" because federation is the precise technical claim |
| "Community-owned" | True only if structurally true (post-foundation); until then we say "foundation-owned" or "open source" |
| "Trustless" | Crypto-speak; we sell trust |

## The competitive comparison rules

When Encore talks about competitors:

1. **Compare on facts, not vibes.** "Bandcamp charges 10–15%, Encore charges 0% on direct sales — processor fee only."
2. **Cite the source for every number.** The four strategy docs have the citations; marketing copy links to them.
3. **Never personally criticize a founder.** We criticize Spotify's $1B Helsing investment as a *structural risk* of investor-funded platforms — not as a personal attack on Daniel Ek.
4. **Acknowledge wins.** SoundCloud's Fan-Powered Royalties is genuinely better than Spotify's pro-rata. Bandcamp built the gold standard for artist payouts pre-2023. We're not pretending competitors are uniformly bad — we're pointing out the structural ceiling each one hits.
5. **Punch up, not down.** We compare to the incumbent giants and the well-funded alts. We do *not* compare ourselves to other early-stage OSS projects (Funkwhale, Navidrome). Those are peers.
